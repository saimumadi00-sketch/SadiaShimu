About

Biography & Summary

I am Mst. Sadia Afrin Shimu, a zoology researcher based in Bangladesh, focused on primate conservation, biodiversity documentation, environmental education, and field-based ecological research.

My work bridges academic inquiry with on-the-ground conservation practice — studying Bengal slow lorises, documenting trafficking threats, raising awareness in forest-adjacent school communities, and contributing to national biodiversity knowledge. I believe conservation starts with education, and education must be rooted in field reality.

I am affiliated with the Department of Zoology, Jagannath University, Dhaka, where I completed my BSc in Zoology and am continuing postgraduate research. My public research profile includes work on Bengal slow loris distribution, conservation outreach, primate trafficking, translocation survival, activity budgets, and home-range monitoring.

Research Portfolio

Research Projects

Current

Population Status & Distribution of Bengal Slow Loris

Conference research focused on Bengal slow loris population status and distribution in Lawachara National Park, northeast Bangladesh, addressing a poorly understood nocturnal primate through field survey work.

Lawachara National Park 2025
Past

Bengal Slow Loris Conservation & Awareness

Conservation-from-the-root awareness program targeting school communities to protect the Endangered Bengal slow loris in Bangladesh. The published outreach study worked with 251 students from four forest-adjacent schools using talks, storytelling, visual learning, and games.

Northeast & southeast Bangladesh 2020-2022 field outreach; 2025 article
Past

Non-Human Primate Trafficking Documentation

Documentation of recent live primate trafficking in Bangladesh using online news sources from January 2021 to June 2024. The study reported 19 trafficking incidents involving 32 primates across ten districts and identified five trafficked species.

Bangladesh 2021-2024 records; 2025 article
Past

Post-release Survival & Home Range — Bengal Slow Loris

Translocation and spatial ecology research on confiscated Bengal slow lorises released into Lawachara National Park. The work used radio telemetry, night field monitoring, focal sampling, GPS locations, KDE home-range estimation, and QGIS visualization to assess survival and release-site risks.

Lawachara National Park 2024 poster; 2026 article
